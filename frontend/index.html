🚫 Typical Viral Hoax

"SHOCKING: Drink lemon water & baking soda to CURE cancer overnight! Forward before deleted!!"

  • ⚠️ Panic Urgency: Demands immediate forwarding before "censorship".
  • ⚠️ Weasel Words: "Secret doctors who cannot be named".
  • ⚠️ No Verification: Zero published studies or named hospital spokespersons.
  • ⚠️ Emotional Traps: Uses ALL-CAPS and extreme miraculous promises.
✅ Authentic News Wire

"World Health Organization prequalifies pediatric malaria vaccine following clinical trials"

  • ✓ Named Attribution: Cites Dr. Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, WHO Director-General.
  • ✓ Primary Citations: Links directly to peer-reviewed data in The Lancet journal.
  • ✓ Measured Tone: Objective language, transparent percentages, and partner details.
  • ✓ Verified Publisher: Published by Reuters with institutional editorial standards.

👵 Safe Sharing Guide: How to Protect Your Family

Simple, straightforward rules to help everyone—including parents and grandparents—avoid falling for viral rumors.

🛑

Rule 1: Look out for "Forwarded many times"

When WhatsApp shows a message has been forwarded many times, it means the original author is unknown. Treat it as a rumor until verified.

⏰

Rule 2: Don't panic if it screams "URGENT"

Scammers use artificial fear to force you to forward without thinking. Real government ministries announce news calmly through official television and press bureaus.

🍋

Rule 3: Miracle kitchen cures are dangerous fakes

Claims that lemon water, salt, or baking soda cure cancer or diabetes in 3 days are dangerous fabrications. Always consult certified physicians.

📞

Rule 4: When in doubt, check Validata

Before sending to friends or family groups, paste the message here or ask a tech-savvy family member to cross-check with official sources.
